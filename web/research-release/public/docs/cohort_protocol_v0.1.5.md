I add a separate supplement containing all four formula-implementation orders in the screened library. The resulting archive contains 27 written decisions, not 27 independently determined cost-of-equity awards. The primary cohort and its eligibility rules are unchanged.</p><p>The added records are GMP 19-1932-TF and Vermont Gas 24-2137-TF, 25-1261-TF and 26-1298-TF. These orders were already preserved and screened. This release admits them to a distinct analytical stratum; it does not claim four newly downloaded originals. Formula applications were excluded under the earlier design because they did not involve a fresh model determination. They are useful for a separate question: how an existing regulatory mechanism changes the return used in rates. Keeping them separate avoids treating a formula application as a new discretionary ROE decision.</p><p>Written decisions fall between January 1, 2019 and October 1, 2026. I include all four screened formula implementations, without choosing them by the size or direction of their returns. The original screening classifications are preserved in a versioned snapshot. The new manifest records the snapshot and every source fingerprint. Decisions without an ROE determination remain excluded. The 0.1.4 manifest, numerical records, screening file and original quota protocol remain unchanged.</p><p>The two new numerical records are Vermont Gas&#x27;s 9.62% in the August 26, 2025 order and 9.63% in the August 7, 2026 order. Each value is explicitly described as a formula adjustment in the order approving the filing. I do not infer a numeric award for the 2024 order from a subsequent order&#x27;s account of the previous return. GMP&#x27;s 2019 order requires a corrected calculation; its quoted departmental recommendation is not entered as an operative numerical award. Missing values remain missing.</p><p>The default tools use only the primary cohort. Readers can select the settlement and formula supplements explicitly. Joint-service decisions remain one document. Shared proceedings remain identified. Repeated annual formula decisions are repeated observations of a mechanism, not independent experiments. State averages pool different years, services and decision processes and cannot identify a jurisdiction effect.</p><p>A separate regional panel uses EEI&#x27;s 2025 Q3 public workbook. It contains 57 completed electric base-rate observations dated 2010 through 2024, of which 46 report an ROE. It includes formula and advice-letter entries under the publisher&#x27;s base-rate categories. Those publisher records are not added to the original-PDF cohort. Each retains its worksheet row, source vintage and missing values. The two datasets overlap and must not be added together as unique orders.</p><p>The six-state census, remaining numerical entry, complete-section coding, independent agreement and strict RRA half-year coverage remain separate completion criteria. Source checks do not establish independent coding agreement. Retrospective EEI comparisons do not fill missing contemporaneous RRA periods. Future additions require another dated manifest.</p></main></body></html>
