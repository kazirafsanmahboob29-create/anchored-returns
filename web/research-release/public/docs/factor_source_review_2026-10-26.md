Its notes identify a change to direct Treasury sourcing beginning June 21, 2019. The term spread describes the Treasury curve, rather than compensation for corporate default risk. <a href="https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/about.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">H.15 release description</a>, <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y3M" rel="noopener noreferrer">term-spread notes</a>.</p><p>STLFSI4 combines 18 financial series, including interest rates, yield spreads and other indicators. Some components overlap conceptually with the Treasury variables, so these regressors should not be described as independent economic shocks. Version 4 was introduced on November 10, 2022 and replaced backward-looking SOFR with forward-looking SOFR in two spreads. Earlier observations in the current file are reconstructed history under that definition. The index is a broad financial-stress measure, rather than a utility credit spread or an equity risk premium. <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/STLFSI4" rel="noopener noreferrer">Series definition</a>, <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/blog/2022/11/the-st-louis-feds-financial-stress-index-version-4/" rel="noopener noreferrer">version 4 methodology</a>.</p><p>CPI measures consumer prices rather than utility construction costs or investors&#x27; expected inflation. For a source reference month <code>m</code>, inflation is <code>100 * (CPI[m] / CPI[m-12] - 1)</code>. For a decision in September 2025, the two-month convention uses July 2025 and July 2024. The 2008 start supplies the earlier observations needed for inflation calculations near the beginning of the 2010 sample. October 2025 is blank in the current download and remains missing. It is outside the current EEI analysis window. <a href="https://www.bls.gov/cpi/questions-and-answers.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS CPI definition and revision policy</a>.</p><h2>Coverage within the EEI analysis window</h2><div class="doc-table"><table><thead><tr><th>Series</th><th>Rows dated January 1, 2010 to September 30, 2025</th><th>Numeric values</th><th>Last observation in window</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>DGS10</td><td>4,108</td><td>3,939</td><td>September 30, 2025</td></tr><tr><td>T10Y3M</td><td>4,108</td><td>3,939</td><td>September 30, 2025</td></tr><tr><td>STLFSI4</td><td>822</td><td>822</td><td>September 26, 2025</td></tr><tr><td>CPIAUCNS</td><td>189</td><td>189</td><td>September 2025</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>Blank daily rows include holidays. Missing observations remain distinct from zero. Weekend dates are generally absent from the daily files. The source manifest records original file hashes, row counts, observation bounds and retrieval timestamps.</p><h2>Timing and vintages</h2><p>An observation date, a publication date and a retrieval date serve different purposes. CPI&#x27;s first-of-month date labels a reference month. STLFSI4&#x27;s Friday date labels a week. Neither date establishes when the value became public. An HTTP <code>Last-Modified</code> header describes the downloaded resource and does not establish the first publication date of every historical observation.</p><p>The two-calendar-month CPI rule and the 14-calendar-day stress rule are conservative lag conventions for a retrospective study. They do not establish historical release availability or remove revisions. A daily Treasury specification also needs an explicit lag and averaging window. Same-day market observations should not be treated as information available before an order whose issuance time is unknown. Daily results should include a longer-lag sensitivity check.</p><p>FRED&#x27;s default historical view uses information available today. ALFRED real-time dates can instead identify information available at a past date. A study claiming historical information sets would need those vintage records, original release dates and a documented intraday convention. They were not obtained in this review. <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/realtime_period.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">FRED real-time documentation</a>.</p><p>The unadjusted CPI-U series is preferable here to seasonally adjusted CPIAUCSL because BLS ordinarily treats unadjusted CPI-U as final when issued, subject to rare corrections. BLS re-estimates seasonally adjusted CPI factors and can revise up to five years of seasonally adjusted history. This preference reduces one source of revision risk but does not prove that every current value equals its original release. <a href="https://www.bls.gov/cpi/questions-and-answers.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS CPI FAQ</a>.</p><h2>Access and file schemas</h2><p>The recommended series downloaded successfully without credentials from the official machine-readable CSV export:</p><p><code>https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=SERIES_ID&amp;cosd=2008-01-01</code></p><p>Each preserved CSV has two columns, <code>observation_date</code> and its series identifier. Dates use <code>YYYY-MM-DD</code>; numeric rates and index levels are decimal strings. Empty values are missing. Importers should also reject or explicitly handle <code>.</code> if a future export uses that missing marker. Data remain at their native frequency and are sorted by observation date.</p><p>This export is distinct from the authenticated FRED observations API. A direct request to <code>api.stlouisfed.org/fred/series/observations</code> without a key returned HTTP 400 and reported that <code>api_key</code> was not set. A free registered key is required for the documented API. No key was supplied, obtained from another application or embedded in the project. The CSV histories are sufficient for the current retrospective extension. <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">FRED observations API</a>.</p><h2>Optional state unemployment</h2><p>The preserved FRED series CTUR, MEUR, MAUR, NHUR, RIUR and VTUR contain monthly, seasonally adjusted unemployment rates for the six New England states. Their units are percentages of the civilian labor force. They describe local labor-market conditions rather than the risk of a specific utility. A two-reference-month lag is a useful retrospective convention. Monthly and annual revisions still prevent an as-known interpretation without historical vintages. <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MAUR" rel="noopener noreferrer">FRED example series</a>, <a href="https://www.bls.gov/lau/laufaq.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS LAUS definitions</a>, <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/laus.tn.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS revision and seasonal-adjustment technical note</a>.</p><p>I also verified the official BLS JSON endpoint, <code>https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v2/timeseries/data/</code>, with two credential-free requests covering 2008-2017 and 2018-2026. Each request contained CPI series CUUR0000SA0 and six state unemployment series. Both returned HTTP 200 and <code>REQUEST_SUCCEEDED</code>. The response retains <code>seriesID</code>, <code>year</code>, <code>period</code>, <code>periodName</code>, <code>value</code> and <code>footnotes</code>, including preliminary flags. Monthly periods are M01 through M12. An M13 annual-average record must not be imported as a month.</p><p>All 1,323 overlapping monthly values from January 2010 through September 2025 matched the separately downloaded FRED files exactly: 189 values for CPI and 189 for each state. This confirms agreement between two retrieval routes for the current data, not independent statistical measurement. BLS documentation gives unregistered access a ten-year query limit and requires registration for expanded version-2 features. The successful keyless requests do not establish entitlement to higher limits or metadata options. <a href="https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_signature_v2.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS API signatures</a>, <a href="https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS access limits</a>.</p><h2>Publication rights</h2><p>FRED labels DGS10, CPIAUCNS and the state unemployment series <code>Public Domain: Citation Requested</code>. It labels STLFSI4 and T10Y3M <code>Copyrighted: Citation Required</code>. The latter two should not be described as public-domain series. FRED&#x27;s summarized terms permit displaying and publishing the citation-required category with source attribution and acknowledgment that the data were obtained through FRED, subject to its terms and prohibited uses. The project should retain the suggested citations and avoid implying Federal Reserve endorsement. <a href="https://fred.stlouisfed.org/legal/" rel="noopener noreferrer">FRED terms and copyright categories</a>.</p><p>Moody&#x27;s AAA, BAA, DAAA, DBAA and BAA10Y histories were accessible for local review, but their series notes include provider-specific reproduction and redistribution restrictions.
