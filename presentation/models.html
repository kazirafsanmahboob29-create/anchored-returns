Timing rules, exclusions and interpretation

Every specification uses cases with an award, all four economic measures, a valid filing duration and a strictly earlier recorded award for the same exact utility-state label. This holds the estimation sample fixed when a predictor is added. The workbench reports the selected period's entries and complete observations separately.

Daily market windows end before authorization. CPI uses the reference month two months earlier and a twelve-month change. Financial stress uses the last observation at least fourteen days earlier. All histories use the retrieved vintage, including later revisions. This is a retrospective model.

Prior awards use the latest earlier authorization date within ten years. Multiple entries on that date are averaged; same-day observations never supply one another's prior award. Utility labels are inherited from the workbook. Persistence can reflect stable risk, policy or formula design as well as reliance on precedent. A lagged dependent variable with utility fixed effects can also have short-panel bias.

State controls account for persistent jurisdiction differences; year controls absorb common annual variation. That can remove much of the variation in national economic series, so a small adjusted Treasury slope is not evidence that interest rates have no effect. Selecting a narrower period can also leave too little independent variation to fit the requested model.

Financial stress is a broad St. Louis Fed index. The yield curve is a Treasury maturity spread. Neither measures a utility's equity risk premium. Analysis specification · Methods and results.
